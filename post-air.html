Air Purifiers, Humidifiers, and Which Numbers Mean Anything

Many devices claim to clean the air, but few actually help with common indoor pollutants. Understanding the numbers separates the useful from the wishful.

The fashionhotspot editors·Updated 12 August 2026·6 min read·10 picks

Indoor air quality is a complicated topic. You cannot see most of the particles, gases, or humidity levels that affect it. This makes it easy for marketers to sell you things that do little. Many products promise cleaner air but lack the fundamental technology or power to make a real difference in a typical home setting. Be sceptical of vague claims and focus on measurable improvements.

The most useful devices address specific problems. Air purifiers with HEPA filters remove particles like dust, pollen, and pet dander. Humidifiers add moisture to overly dry air, which can help with comfort. Dehumidifiers remove excess moisture, critical for preventing mold. Air quality monitors give you data, so you know what you are actually dealing with before you buy anything.

Common questions

What do CADR, MERV, and PM2.5 numbers mean?

CADR, or Clean Air Delivery Rate, tells you how quickly an air purifier cleans a room of specific particles (pollen, dust, smoke). Higher numbers mean faster cleaning. MERV, Minimum Efficiency Reporting Value, applies to HVAC filters; higher MERV means the filter traps smaller particles, but can also restrict airflow. PM2.5 refers to particulate matter 2.5 micrometers in diameter or smaller. These are fine particles that can easily enter lungs. Air quality monitors track PM2.5 levels, giving you data on airborne particle pollution in your space.

Are 'ionic' or 'ozone' air purifiers effective?

Generally, no. Be very wary of products that claim to 'ionize' or 'ozonate' the air. Ozone is a lung irritant at levels high enough to clean air effectively. Manufacturers often market these devices with vague claims, but they often produce ozone as a byproduct. While some ionic purifiers can reduce particles, they are typically less effective than HEPA filters and can come with health risks from ozone. For safe and effective particle removal, stick to true HEPA filtration.

How often should I change filters?

It depends on the device, air quality, and usage. For air purifiers, HEPA filters typically last 6-12 months, and carbon pre-filters every 3-6 months. Humidifier wicks need changing every 1-3 months, as they trap minerals and can grow mold. HVAC filters (like MERV 8-13) usually need replacement every 1-3 months. Neglecting filter changes reduces efficiency, can circulate pollutants, or even damage the device. Always check the manufacturer's recommendations and factor filter costs into your budget.

Can plants clean my indoor air?

While plants do absorb carbon dioxide and release oxygen, their ability to clean indoor air of pollutants like volatile organic compounds (VOCs) is very limited in a typical home environment. Research, including some NASA studies, showed plants could remove certain chemicals in sealed chambers. However, the scale of that effect does not translate to a normal room with air circulation. You would need hundreds of plants in a single room to see a measurable reduction in common indoor air pollutants. Buy plants for aesthetics and enjoyment, not as a primary air purification solution.
