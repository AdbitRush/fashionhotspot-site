Running Kit for People Who Go Out in the Rain

Equip yourself for outdoor running with items that last, perform reliably, and justify their cost.

The fashionhotspot editors10 picks

Running outdoors is a simple act, but the gear market often complicates it. Many runners begin with basic clothes, only to find them uncomfortable or inadequate in varying weather. The appeal of specialized kit is strong, promising enhanced performance or comfort, yet much of it offers marginal gains for significant expense. It is easy to accumulate items that prove unnecessary or ill-suited to your actual running conditions.

The trap lies in believing every new product offers a substantial improvement. Marketing often highlights features that sound impressive but translate poorly to real-world use. Before buying, consider what problems you genuinely need to solve: staying visible, managing sweat, or keeping warm. Prioritize durability and practicality over flashy aesthetics or a long list of technical terms. Focus on pieces that consistently perform their intended job, mile after mile.

Common questions

Do I really need specialized running clothes, or can I just use my regular gym wear?

For occasional, short runs, regular gym wear is often fine. However, dedicated running apparel is designed to wick sweat away from your body, preventing chafing and helping to regulate temperature. Cotton, for instance, absorbs sweat and stays wet, which can lead to discomfort, blisters, and chills in cooler weather. Investing in a few key technical pieces, like a good shirt and socks, will significantly improve comfort and performance for regular outdoor running. You do not need a full wardrobe of high-end gear, but a few functional items are worth considering.

How important is waterproofing for jackets when running outdoors, and what are the trade-offs?

Full waterproofing is critical if you expect to run in heavy, continuous rain. However, truly waterproof jackets tend to be less breathable, trapping sweat and heat inside, which can make you feel clammy. For most runners, a water-resistant windbreaker that sheds light rain and protects against wind is a more versatile choice. These jackets offer better breathability and are often lighter and more packable. Unless you regularly run in downpours, prioritizing breathability and wind protection over full waterproofing might be a more practical approach for comfort on the run.

Is a GPS watch worth the money, or can my phone do the same job for tracking runs?

Your smartphone, combined with a running app, can effectively track your distance, pace, and route using its built-in GPS. For many, this is perfectly adequate. A dedicated GPS watch, however, offers convenience; you do not need to carry your phone, and data is visible at a glance on your wrist without fumbling. Watches also tend to have more accurate GPS, offer advanced metrics like heart rate without a chest strap, and have longer battery life for tracking. If you are serious about training and want detailed, real-time data, a watch can be beneficial. Otherwise, your phone is a capable alternative.

What about hydration packs versus carrying a handheld bottle for water on longer runs?

For shorter runs where you only need a small amount of water, a handheld bottle is simple and convenient. It is easy to fill and clean, and you can quickly take a sip. However, carrying a bottle for longer distances can become tiresome, and some find the shifting weight distracting. Hydration vests distribute the weight across your back and shoulders, offering a more balanced and hands-free experience. They also typically allow you to carry more water and other essentials. The trade-off is the vest's higher cost, more complex cleaning, and potential for feeling warm or bulky, especially if not packed well.
