Wrapping and Posting Supplies That Actually Work

Avoid the annual present-wrapping scramble with tools and materials that simplify the process, from paper to postage to year-after-year storage.

The fashionhotspot editors10 picks

The end of the year brings a familiar cycle: the rush to buy presents, followed by the less glamorous but equally urgent task of wrapping and sending them. It often feels like a hurried afterthought, leading to frustration with flimsy paper, tangled tape, and last-minute runs to the post office for inadequate packaging. This guide focuses on the practical, unglamorous items that genuinely make this necessary chore less painful.

The trap here is buying into the idea that wrapping needs to be an artistic endeavor every single year. While a beautiful presentation is nice, functional, durable materials that protect the contents and make the job easier are more valuable. This means investing in reliable tools and basic supplies that you can reuse or easily restock, rather than single-use decorative items that add clutter and stress without real utility.

Common questions

Do I need special, fancy wrapping paper?

Probably not. While highly decorative paper can look impressive, it often costs more and can be less durable than simpler options. Many specialty papers are also not recyclable due to coatings, glitter, or foil. Plain brown kraft paper or solid-color rolls are often more economical, more versatile, and easier to recycle. You can achieve a personalized look with simple twine, stamps, or a handwritten tag, which often feels more genuine than an elaborate pre-printed design. Focus on paper that's thick enough not to tear easily, rather than just its appearance.

Is a tape gun dispenser really worth it?

If you are only wrapping one or two gifts, a basic desktop or handheld tape dispenser is sufficient. However, if you regularly send multiple packages or seal several boxes for storage, a tape gun can significantly speed up the process and reduce hand fatigue. It allows for quick, continuous application of wide packing tape, creating secure seals much faster than tearing individual strips. Consider your volume: if you dread the annual box-sealing marathon, it might be a worthwhile investment to save time and frustration.

What about decorative bows and ribbons?

Decorative bows and ribbons are entirely optional and often single-use. While they can add a festive touch, they are also prone to getting crushed in transit, and many are made from non-recyclable materials. If you want to add embellishment, consider using natural twine, paper ribbon, or reusable fabric ribbons that can be repurposed. The priority for posting presents should be secure packaging and clear labeling. For gifts given in person, a simple, durable ribbon can still elevate the presentation without unnecessary complexity or waste. Don't feel obligated to buy elaborate, single-use decorations.

Should I buy a dedicated label printer for shipping?

For most people sending gifts a few times a year, a standard inkjet or laser printer with adhesive label sheets is perfectly adequate. A dedicated thermal label printer, while fast and efficient, is a significant investment and primarily benefits those who ship dozens or hundreds of packages regularly. The cost of the printer and specialized labels might not be justified for occasional use. If your existing home printer can reliably handle label sheets, stick with that. The key is legibility and proper adhesion, which standard printers can achieve well with the right labels.
