Commuting by Bike Through a Bad Winter

Equip your bike for daily rides, from securing it to staying dry, avoiding unnecessary purchases.

The fashionhotspot editors10 picks

Cycling to work or for errands seems simple. It is not always. The bike itself is one thing, but the gear you need to make it reliable and practical is another. Many newcomers buy too much, or the wrong things. This guide focuses on the essentials that make daily cycling feasible, even enjoyable, without unnecessary expense or complication. We prioritised items that directly address common commuting problems.

The trap is convenience. Manufacturers want to sell you a product for every conceivable niche. You do not need most of them. A good bike setup for commuting is often about minimalism and durability. Each item chosen here has a clear purpose and helps avoid common frustrations like flat tires, theft, or arriving at your destination wet. Buy what you need, use it often, and ignore the rest.

Common questions

Do I really need two lights, front and rear?

Yes, for commuting, two lights are important. A front light helps you see the road and makes you visible from the front. A separate rear light ensures you are seen by traffic approaching from behind. Relying on one light, or a weak one, significantly compromises your safety. Modern lights are small, rechargeable, and easy to mount, so there is little reason not to use both. Think of them as essential safety equipment, not optional extras, especially if you ride near traffic or in low light conditions.

Is a U-lock always better than a chain lock?

Not always, but generally for urban commuting, a U-lock like the Kryptonite KryptoLok offers a good blend of security and portability. Chain locks can be very secure, but they are often much heavier and bulkier for the same level of protection. A U-lock secures your frame and one wheel effectively to an immovable object. If you need to secure multiple bikes or awkward objects, a chain might be more versatile, but for single bike security, the U-lock is usually the more practical choice for daily transport.

Should I get a bike computer or just use my phone for navigation?

For most commuters, your phone is sufficient. Many apps provide good navigation and can track your ride data. A dedicated bike computer adds cost and another device to charge. Phone mounts are inexpensive, and most phones are already waterproof enough for light rain. Save your money on a bike computer unless you are training seriously, or frequently ride very long distances where phone battery life becomes a major concern. The phone you already own is often the best solution here.

What is the most important item if I can only buy one thing?

If you can only buy one item beyond the bike itself, it should be a reliable lock. Without a good lock, your bike is vulnerable to theft the moment you leave it unattended. Lights are also critical for safety, but a stolen bike ends your commute entirely. Invest in a solid U-lock first. Then, once your bike is secure, consider front and rear lights to ensure you are seen and can see your path. Prioritise keeping your bike yours.
