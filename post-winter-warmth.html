Being Warm at Home Without Heating the Whole House

Stay warm and save money by targeting heat where you need it, rather than turning up the thermostat for the whole house.

The fashionhotspot editors10 picks

Heating an entire house is expensive, especially in colder months. Energy costs have made many people reconsider how they approach keeping comfortable indoors. Instead of raising the thermostat for every room, focusing warmth on the person or specific area where it is needed can be a more efficient and economical strategy. This approach can lead to significant savings on utility bills while still providing adequate warmth.

The market for personal and localized heating solutions is wide, but not every product offers good value or effective performance. Many options promise comfort but deliver little more than novelty or consume excessive power for the warmth they provide. Understanding the trade-offs of each category – from low-tech insulation to electric appliances – is key to making choices that genuinely help you stay warm and reduce your energy consumption.

Common questions

What is the cheapest way to stay warm at home?

The absolute cheapest ways involve no electricity. Layering clothing, using a hot water bottle, and actively draught-proofing your home are all free or low-cost methods. A simple fabric door snake can block significant cold air. If you need targeted heat, an electric throw blanket is very efficient for personal warmth, costing only a few cents per hour. Avoiding heating an entire empty room is key; focus warmth on yourself with blankets or localized heating only when and where you are present. These passive and low-power options are often more effective than trying to warm a large space.

Are portable electric heaters safe to use?

Portable electric heaters can be safe if used correctly and if they meet recognized safety standards. Always look for a safety certification mark from a reputable testing laboratory, such as UL (Underwriters Laboratories) or ETL (Intertek), which indicates the product has been tested to national safety standards. Place heaters on a stable, non-combustible surface, away from anything flammable like curtains, furniture, or bedding. Never plug a heater into an extension cord or power strip, as this can overload circuits and cause fires. Ensure models have tip-over switches and overheat protection for added safety.

How much does it cost to run a heated blanket compared to a room heater?

A heated blanket or mattress pad is significantly cheaper to run than a room heater. Most heated blankets draw between 60-150 watts. At an average electricity cost of 15 cents per kilowatt-hour, a 100-watt blanket costs about 1.5 cents per hour to run. In contrast, most portable room heaters, whether oil-filled or ceramic fan models, typically draw 1000-1500 watts. Running a 1500-watt heater costs approximately 22.5 cents per hour. The difference is substantial, making personal heated items a much more economical choice for individual warmth than trying to heat a whole room.

Should I buy a smart thermostat for a single cold room?

Buying a full smart thermostat system for a single cold room is likely an overcomplication and often not worth the expense. Smart thermostats are primarily designed for whole-house heating systems, providing control over a central furnace or boiler. For a single cold room, simpler and more direct solutions are usually better. A heated throw blanket or a small, targeted portable electric heater that you can turn on and off manually or with a simple timer is often more effective and significantly less expensive. A smart thermostat for one room might only control a single zone if your home's HVAC system supports it, which is rare for add-on solutions.
