Bathroom Fixes a Landlord Cannot Object To

Practical bathroom upgrades and fixes for renters and homeowners, focusing on utility over trends.

The fashionhotspot editors·Updated 12 August 2026·7 min read·10 picks

Bathrooms, especially smaller or rented ones, often present a unique challenge: making them functional and pleasant without permanent changes. It is easy to accumulate items that promise to solve all your problems but end up contributing to clutter. The goal should be to introduce purposeful pieces that genuinely improve your daily routine or address specific issues like limited storage or an outdated shower. Avoid impulse buys that offer minor aesthetic tweaks without tangible benefits.

Many products in the bathroom category leverage convenience as their main selling point, which can be misleading. A quick fix might seem appealing, but if it breaks easily, doesn't actually solve the problem, or makes the space less usable, it is a waste of money. Focus on solutions that are robust, adaptable, and offer a clear improvement to your space or routine. Prioritize items that can be easily removed or repurposed, particularly in a rental scenario.

Common questions

Are expensive towels actually worth the price, or is it just marketing?

For most people, very expensive towels are not a necessary upgrade. While high-end towels might feature longer-staple cottons or unique weaves that feel softer initially, this difference often diminishes significantly after a few washes. Their primary job is to absorb water, and many mid-range cotton or even microfiber towels do that effectively. If you prioritize durability and basic absorbency, a moderately priced set will serve you well. Instead of chasing the softest feel, look for towels with a good balance of absorbency and reasonable drying time, which are usually available at a lower price point. Don't overspend here.

Can I really install a new shower head in a rental bathroom without causing problems?

Yes, in most cases, replacing a shower head in a rental is a simple, reversible upgrade. Shower heads typically unscrew from the shower arm with a wrench or even by hand. You simply twist off the old one, wrap some plumber's tape around the threads of the shower arm (usually included with new shower heads), and screw on the new one. Keep the original shower head in a safe place so you can reattach it before moving out. This modification leaves no permanent marks or changes, making it a landlord-friendly update that can significantly improve your shower experience.

What's the best way to deal with mildew and grime in a rented bathroom that isn't sparkling clean?

Start with consistent cleaning using appropriate bathroom cleaners. For mildew, products containing bleach or hydrogen peroxide can be effective, but always test in an inconspicuous area first and ensure good ventilation. For general grime, an all-purpose bathroom cleaner and a stiff brush will often do the trick. If grout is discolored but structurally sound, a grout pen can offer a cosmetic refresh. Remember to ventilate the bathroom after showering to prevent new mildew growth. While you can't renovate, thorough cleaning and strategic cosmetic fixes can significantly improve the space.

How can I add more storage to a small bathroom without drilling or making permanent changes?

Focus on vertical and over-fixture solutions. An over-the-door towel rack or a tension rod shower caddy can add substantial storage without tools. Under-sink organizers are excellent for maximizing cabinet space around plumbing. For smaller items, consider suction cup caddies for the shower or adhesive hooks on smooth wall surfaces. Freestanding shelves or narrow rolling carts can fit into unused corners. The key is to look for items that are self-supporting or use temporary, damage-free adhesion, allowing for easy removal when you move or want to reconfigure the space.
